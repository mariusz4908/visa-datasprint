Card Payment Gap Analysis

Cross-referencing Visa transaction data (305M transactions sample) with GUS household expenditure structure to identify spending categories where card payments are underutilized in Poland
VISA DATASPRINT HACKATHON 2026

Methodology

Approach: We compare the structure (% share) of real household spending from GUS Household Budget Survey (COICOP classification, 2024) with the structure of Visa card transactions by merchant category (MCC codes). The Visa data is a sample — absolute numbers don't represent total market size, but the proportional distribution across categories reveals where cards are over- or under-represented relative to actual consumer spending.

Deep Dive: The 5 Biggest Card-Free Zones

1. Housing & Utilities

  • 20.6% of spending but <0.5% of card transactions
  • Rent: paid by bank transfer (standing orders)
  • Electricity, gas, water: direct debit or transfer
  • Mortgage payments: automated bank deductions
  • Only 469K utility card transactions found in entire dataset
  • Gap Index: 2 (virtually zero card penetration)
  • Opportunity: Card-linked bill payment, utility autopay via card

2. Healthcare Services

  • 5.5% of spending but ~2.5% of card value
  • Pharmacies (Drug Stores): well covered (8.8M tx)
  • Doctor visits: only 255K tx (0.08%) — cash dominates
  • Hospitals: only 99K tx (0.03%)
  • Dental: 76K tx + 35K lab tx
  • Gap Index: 46 (half the expected card usage)
  • Opportunity: Terminal deployment in clinics, dental offices, physiotherapy

3. Communications / Telecom

  • 4.0% of spending but <0.1% of card transactions
  • Phone bills: direct debit / bank transfer
  • Internet subscriptions: same pattern
  • Prepaid top-ups: BLIK or transfer
  • Only 16 "Visaphone" transactions in entire dataset
  • Gap Index: ~1 (effectively zero)
  • Opportunity: Card-on-file for recurring telecom payments

4. Education

  • 1.1% of spending but <0.03% of card transactions
  • Schools/universities: 25K tx (tuition by transfer)
  • Tutoring/private lessons: mostly cash
  • Nurseries/childcare: 16.6K tx total
  • Books/school supplies: 957K tx (better covered)
  • Gap Index: 3
  • Opportunity: School fee payment platforms, edtech card integration

5. Home Furnishings & Maintenance

  • 4.4% of spending but ~2.6% of card value
  • Home supply stores: 2.7M tx (Castorama, Leroy Merlin)
  • Furniture stores: 656K tx
  • But: home repair/plumber/electrician = cash
  • Cleaning services: 27K tx only
  • Household repair services: 31K tx
  • Gap Index: 59
  • Opportunity: Service marketplace with card payments (like TaskRabbit)

6. Alcohol & Tobacco

  • 2.5% of spending but ~0.8% of card value
  • Package stores (beer/wine/liquor): 1.6M tx
  • Cigar stores: 880K tx
  • But: kiosks, small vendors, corner shops = cash-heavy
  • Nightclub/bar cash payments not captured
  • Gap Index: 32
  • Opportunity: Terminal rollout to small kiosks and tobacco shops

Where Cards ARE Well Used (Overrepresented)

Categories with Gap Index > 100 (card overrepresentation)
Key observations on well-covered categories
Food & Groceries (GUS: 27.1% | Visa value: 25.3%)
Near-perfect match. 80M supermarket + 47M convenience store transactions. Cards are the default payment method for grocery shopping in Poland. Gap Index: 93 (slightly under due to small shops/markets using cash).
Restaurants & Hotels (GUS: 5.7% | Visa value: 9.6%)
Overrepresented — Gap Index: 168. Card usage in gastronomy exceeds the GUS spending share. Possible reasons: business meals, tourist spending, higher-value dining tends to use cards. 30.2M restaurant + fast food transactions.
Transport (GUS: 9.1% | Visa value: 7.6%)
Well covered. 16.1M fuel station + 4.2M taxi + 3.8M public transit + 2.8M parking transactions. Gap Index: 84. The gap comes from car purchases (financed separately) and car insurance (bank transfer).
Clothing (GUS: 4.4% | Visa value: 5.6%)
Overrepresented — Gap Index: 127. Fashion retail has fully adopted card payments. Online fashion (Allegro, Zalando) drives up the card share. 9.9M clothing-related transactions.

Strategic Conclusions

The Recurring Payments Opportunity

  • Housing (20.6%) + Communications (4.0%) = 24.6% of all spending is invisible to cards
  • These are recurring, predictable payments — ideal for card-on-file
  • In markets like UK/US, utility and telecom payments on card are common
  • Poland's bank transfer infrastructure is excellent, creating a high switching cost
  • Strategy: Partner with utility providers for card-on-file autopay with cashback incentives. Target the ~14.5M households each paying ~416 PLN/month on housing + 68 PLN on telecom

The Cash Economy in Services

  • Doctor visits (255K tx), dental (76K tx), home repairs (31K tx) are disproportionately low
  • These are high-value, in-person transactions — exactly where cash persists
  • Private medical sector in Poland is large (~40% of healthcare spending)
  • Plumbers, electricians, tutors = gray economy + cash preference
  • Strategy: Terminal deployment programs for medical clinics, dental offices. Partner with service platforms (Fixly, Oferteo) for card payment integration

The E-Commerce Card Gap

  • E-commerce is 9.5% of retail but BLIK dominates online payments in Poland
  • Groceries: 99.9% physical, 0.1% online — e-grocery is nascent
  • Pharmacies: 99.8% physical — e-pharmacy growing but tiny
  • Online clothing avg transaction: 646 vs 179 in-store (3.6x higher)
  • Strategy: Position Visa as preferred payment for e-grocery and e-pharmacy growth. Online transactions have higher average value = more revenue per transaction

The ATM Indicator

  • 5.4M ATM transactions with avg withdrawal of 1,521 (8.3x avg card tx)
  • ATM cash = 14.7% of all transaction value in the dataset
  • This cash flows into card-free zones: rent, doctors, markets, services
  • High ATM avg suggests deliberate cash-for-purpose withdrawals, not pocket money
  • Strategy: Track ATM usage patterns to identify where cash is flowing. Correlate high-ATM areas with low-terminal-density areas. Target these zones for merchant acquisition
